The 0.1 ms TCP connect never measured the network the page used. This paragraph used to argue that the guest was CPU-starved rather than slow, because Page.enable cost 6.8 ms on 2 vCPUs against 3.9 on 4 while "TCP connect is 0.1 ms in every run". That connect is stages.tcp_ms, which cdpdrive.py defines as its own TCP connect to the WebSocket endpoint: the harness on the host reaching the guest’s forwarded CDP port on 127.0.0.2:9222 over loopback. It says nothing about the page’s connections to its origins. Those are in the same records under render.nav, and they contradict the conclusion: in the withdrawn 4 vCPU run dns_ms is nonzero in 8 of 460 render records, seven of them between 149 and 158 ms, and ttfb_ms p99 is 279 ms with a 3.6 s maximum, against 4.6 and 4.9 ms and a 15.0 ms maximum in the DNS-verified run. The renders were waiting on the live internet. The conclusion is withdrawn; a CPU-starvation reading needs a ladder on the fixed tree with render.nav quoted beside Page.enable. The six-URL direction argument that stood here, built from the withdrawn rungs because no verified ladder existed, is superseded by the ladder below and removed. On that ladder render.nav.dns_ms is zero in all 230 render records of every rung, with ttfb_ms maxima of 15.1, 2.5 and 2.2 ms. The CPU-starvation reading is still not re-derived, because Page.enable orders the wrong way for it: 2.5 ms at 2 vCPU, 2.8 at 4 and 3.8 at 8, so the stage gets slower as cores are added, not faster. The spread between runs also exceeds the effect: the other verified 2 vCPU run reads 4.3 ms against this ladder’s 2.5, a 1.9 ms gap at one vCPU count, wider than the 1.4 ms the ladder spans from 2 to 8. The stage does not discriminate.

The knee is between 2 and 4 vCPU; 4 to 8 does not separate. 549.4 ms is 28.7% below 770.3, a step of 220.9 ms, and all 14 URLs are faster at 4 than at 2. From 4 to 8 each rung’s median sits inside the other’s interval, and 8 vCPU is slower than 4 on 11 of the 14 URLs, faster only on the three heaviest (elmundo 2,907.2 against 2,945.2 ms, rtp.pt 1,945.8 against 2,195.0, theguardian 894.9 against 913.7). So 4 vCPU is the operating point on latency. What the ladder does not price is density: twice the vCPUs per render halves how many renders a fixed core budget holds concurrently, and no run here measures that.

Read the intervals as within-run: 202 requests of one run, not run-to-run variance. The 2 and 4 rungs’ intervals do overlap, between 596.2 and 632.4 ms; what separates that pair from 4 against 8 is that neither median falls inside the other’s interval. The 4-to-8 difference is also not in the render: navigate falls 301.0 to 267.2 ms and screenshot 67.1 to 64.1, while resolve rises 153.1 to 204.6, which is a poll rather than a lookup: resolve_target GETs http://{cdp_host}/json/list until the answer carries a page target, sleeping RESOLVE_RETRY_S = 0.05 between attempts (cdpdrive.py:107,184), and cdp_host is the IP literal 127.0.0.2:9222 in every record, so nothing is name-resolved and Chromium inside the guest answers the GET over the same forwarded loopback path as tcp_ms. The stage therefore advances in 50 ms steps: render.resolve_attempts has median 4, 4 and 5 across the rungs, and pooled over the four verified runs the median resolve_ms is 104.1 ms at 3 attempts, 153.6 at 4, 204.6 at 5 and 254.4 at 6, leaving 3.1, 3.2 and 4.5 ms of non-sleep time per rung once the sleeps are subtracted. The 51.5 ms is one extra poll, and mean attempts of 3.58, 3.97 and 4.95 show the whole distribution shifting rather than a boundary being crossed. How much later the 8 vCPU endpoint was ready is not measured: the stage resolves nothing finer than its 50 ms quantum and spans host and guest with no field separating the wait from the request. The box was not equally quiet at every rung: load_max_1min reads 2.62, 18.3 and 16.97 at 2, 4 and 8 vCPU, against 2.87 in the 2026-08-30 run. The gate is checked at run start, and the noop arm, the lifecycle baseline the analyzer rejects a run on when it drifts, holds at 44.9, 45.1 and 45.8 ms across the three rungs. The two verified 2 vCPU cells are 57.7 ms apart: this ladder’s 770.3 and the 2026-08-30 run’s 712.6, on different goldens, fcvm binaries and host boots (cell.host_boot_id 291f8bad and 21ffa582). Their within-run intervals overlap, which says nothing about run-to-run variation: no configuration here was measured twice under the same conditions, so nothing in this report prices that term. What the pair does bound is the spread between two 2 vCPU runs, 57.7 ms, against the 220.9 ms step from 2 to 4 vCPU inside the ladder. Both ran on kernel 6.17.0-1019-aws on aarch64, and the 2026-08-30 record carries no hostinfo.json, so whether that is one box rebooted or two is not established.

Guest vCPU count is baked into the golden, so the three points were three goldens, and the spread this paragraph used to bound them with (916.9, 951.2 and 982.9 ms at 2 vCPU, at least 66 ms) was measured under live DNS and is withdrawn. Each point of a ladder is one gated run of 202 requests, which is 202 samples but a single experimental unit. The confidence intervals the harness reports are within-run and do not carry run-to-run variance. Five independent bursts per configuration, with burst-level intervals, is what would let the ladder’s medians carry real error bars, and that campaign has not been run. The 2026-09-02 ladder is one burst per rung.

The service being measured

One request: restore a clone from a golden snapshot, render one page and capture a screenshot (Page.captureScreenshot) through Chromium's CDP endpoint, driven from the host over a forwarded port, then destroy the clone. Requests share nothing except the read-only snapshot.RB-uffd / RB-file analysis.json arms.exec.blocking_ms.median

Terms used throughout. A golden snapshot is a saved VM image (memory, disk, device state) taken once, at a point where headless Chromium is booted, warmed up (JIT and network service exercised), and idle; every request restores a copy-on-write clone of it. copy and minor are the two userfaultfd modes the memory server uses to fill a clone's RAM on first touch: copy duplicates each faulting 4 KB page into the clone's private memory (UFFDIO_COPY); minor keeps the snapshot in a shared in-memory file and maps the existing page into the clone (UFFDIO_CONTINUE), so clones share clean pages. Hugepages backs guest RAM with 2 MB pages instead of 4 KB, 512× fewer mappings. Prefetch replay: the server records which pages previous requests touched and pre-populates them on restore, off the critical path. Runs measure up to four arms (exec is optional from the NC run onward, which schedules three): cdp (render over CDP, normal teardown), cdp-fast (same render, teardown overlapped with the response), exec (the retired in-guest driver, kept for continuity), and noop (restore, boot, and teardown with no render; the VM-lifecycle baseline, which also serves as a drift canary: its latency should be constant, and the analyzer rejects any run where it is not). Same-golden means two runs restored the identical snapshot; absolute numbers vary ~25% between goldens, so only same-golden pairs are quoted as A/B deltas. Guests are 2 vCPU / 1024 MiB unless stated.

Two workloads. The headline workload is Cloudflare's published 14-URL corpus, byte-replayed locally and rendered in the guest — the CV run, first results section below. The mechanism workload, used for every A/B and optimization comparison, is medium.html, a deterministic fixture (1.4 KB HTML, 14-rule stylesheet, 806 B of real DOM-mutating JS whose execution the DOM dump verifies, four generated PNGs ≈375 KB) served in-guest with Cache-Control: no-store, so every render re-fetches and cold-compiles everything; its byte-identical renders are what make small configuration deltas measurable. The golden's warm point renders a different document and navigates to about:blank before the snapshot; the warm page shares zero byte-resources with the measured pages (enforced by test_fixture_isolation.py). The PF, CPU, and hugepage runs use goldens with that isolation; the earlier runs predate it and carry a disclosed caveat (their warm point had preloaded the site's shared stylesheet and one image).pages/, entry.sh warm point, test_fixture_isolation.py

  • Fidelity: we run unmodified Chromium (full web compatibility, video, WebGL, real TLS). Kitesurf's rebuilt engine does not implement all of these; their post lists the limitations.
  • Workloads: theirs is real pages over the real network; ours renders their 14-site corpus byte-replayed locally (equivalence-checked: 12/14 sites render identically under fail-closed gates; the 2 news sites are content-equivalent, with differences confined to ad/consent request chains the replay leaves unanswered).   • Memory: minor-mode clones share clean snapshot pages and privatize pages they write. That mechanism does not establish a memory advantage; a comparison needs the shared serve, page cache and private clone memory counted on a reconciled basis.

The Firecracker build

The benchmark binary builds from ejc3/firecracker, branch bump-vsock-max-connections: upstream main as of 2026-02-17 plus five commits, which are four changes: the serial-interrupt entry below is a fix plus its regression test. Two of the four fix bugs that upstream has since fixed independently — in both cases after, and crediting, these reports — but this binary's base predates those upstream fixes, so the fork patches remain load-bearing here. Ranked by relevance:

  • UffdMinor memory backend: guest RAM maps MAP_PRIVATE over a memfd the fcvm memory server passes across the UFFD handshake, registered for minor faults. This enables the minor-mode configurations measured here at both page granularities. Only copy mode works without it.
  • vsock muxer connection limit 1023 → 16384 (fork-only; not filed upstream) — every clone's status and exec channels ride vsock, and per-request clone churn burns through connections.
  • THRE serial interrupt on snapshot restore (regression test) — restored guests' serial console; diagnostics correctness, not performance. Reported upstream as #5730; upstream resolved it with maintainer-authored UART state save/restore (#5764, merged 2026-03-31, after this base).
  • dump_dirty file-cursor alignment — snapshot-writing correctness on the golden-creation path. Reported upstream as #5696; upstream shipped its own fix in v1.14.2 (2026-02-27, after this base), crediting the report.

One essential property deserves precision. Restored clones need their monotonic clock to continue from the snapshot instant, or every armed timer fires at once. The interrupt storm that makes restores look 25–300× slower (#630) requires two ingredients that exist only in this project's nested-virtualization fork: a virtual-EL2 guest whose saved register list contains CNTVOFF_EL2 (replayed after the counter registers, clobbering the kernel's offset adjustment), and NV2's trap-and-emulate EL1 timers as the re-fire amplifier. The fix — Firecracker owning the VM-wide offset via KVM_ARM_SET_COUNTER_OFFSET — lives only in that fork; upstream has never used that ioctl on any path, and the fork's upstream submission of it is prepared but not yet opened. The benchmark binary is upstream-based with no NV2 support, so neither ingredient exists: its guests are plain EL1, and what keeps their clock continuous on restore is the host kernel, not Firecracker code — since Marc Zyngier's March 2023 kvm-arm series (KVM_CAP_COUNTER_OFFSET, writable counter registers), KVM recomputes the VM-wide counter offset when snapshot restore replays the saved counter registers, so armed timers stay coherent by construction. Upstream's own use of the capability is boot-time counter reset (Egor Lazarchuk, Jan 2025), which zero-bases fresh VMs and is orthogonal to restore. Twelve gated runs of storm-free restores are consistent with this mechanism.

Bottom line

On the byte-replayed 14-URL corpus, per-request-isolated Chromium has a DNS-verified mix median of 549.4 ms [467.9–632.4] at 4 guest vCPUs, with 202 measured requests and zero failures. The same ladder reads 770.3 ms at 2 vCPU and 580.2 ms at 8; a second verified 2 vCPU run reads 712.6 ms. These are within-run results on separate goldens, not a measurement of production throughput, memory density or whole-system CPU. The fixture experiments remain optimisation evidence. Missing historical records and the withdrawn 2026-08-16 series contribute no current conclusion.
